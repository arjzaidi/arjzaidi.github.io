1. Data collection methods

MethodMeaning
Primary data collectionCollected from scratch — you collected it, or assigned someone to collect it
Secondary data collectionExisting data — already published or available; someone else collected it

Most GIS projects combine both: secondary data (e.g. satellite imagery, census maps) provides the base layers, while primary data (e.g. field measurements, GPS points) adds the site-specific detail.

Think about it

Which of the two methods is more expensive and time-consuming? Which one gives you more control over data quality?

2. Field measurements and GPS

GPS (Global Positioning System) is a constellation of satellites that transmit signals to receivers on the ground. A receiver’s position is estimated based on range measurements — the distance from the receiver to several satellites.

GPS range formula

Range = c × (t / 2)   where   c = 299,792,458 m/s (speed of light) and t = round-trip travel time of the signal.

Worked example — computing a GPS range

Worked example

A GPS receiver measures a round-trip signal travel time of 0.02 seconds to a satellite. How far away is the satellite?

1. What is the range to the satellite?

≈ 2,997,925 m (≈ 3,000 km). Range = 299,792,458 × (0.02 / 2) = 299,792,458 × 0.01 ≈ 2,997,925 m. The signal travels to the satellite and back, so we halve the round-trip time.

2. Why do we divide the travel time by 2?

Because t is the round-trip time — the signal goes from the satellite to the receiver and back. The one-way distance is half of that.

3. How many satellites does a receiver typically need, and why?

At least 4 satellites: three to fix the position in 3D space (x, y, z) and a fourth to correct for the receiver clock’s offset from the satellite clocks.

3. Remote sensing

Definition

Remote sensing is measuring electromagnetic energy reflected or emitted from objects — using airborne or satellite-based instruments.

Satellites vary in many ways, and the choice of satellite depends on what you need to measure:

CharacteristicWhat it means
OrbitPath of the satellite around the Earth (e.g. polar, geostationary)
AltitudeHeight above the Earth’s surface
ResolutionSmallest ground area visible in one pixel (spatial), plus spectral, temporal and radiometric resolution
Revisit frequencyHow often the satellite passes over the same location
Steering capabilityAbility to point the sensor off-nadir to revisit a target sooner
Swath widthWidth of the ground strip imaged in one pass
Image sizeSize of each scene delivered
Stereo capabilityAbility to image the same area from two angles for elevation extraction
Wavelengths collectedWhich parts of the electromagnetic spectrum the sensor records (visible, NIR, SWIR, thermal, radar…)
Other sensorsAdditional instruments on the same platform
Delivery timeHow quickly data reaches the user after acquisition

There is no single “best” satellite — you trade off resolution, revisit frequency, swath width and cost depending on your application.

4. Data collection protocols

When you collect your own (primary) data, follow these protocols:

  • Employ a reliable sampling method for representative data.
  • Standardize procedures and employ quality control to enhance data reliability and consistency.
  • Minimize bias in primary data collection.
  • Obtain consent and safeguard participants’ confidentiality and rights.

5. Data use protocols

When you use existing (secondary) data, follow these protocols:

  • Credit secondary data sources through proper citation.
  • Ensure data is obtained and used ethically.
  • Adhere to pertinent laws and regulations — legal conformity, intellectual property rights and ethical standards.
  • Assess the quality and reliability of data before utilization.
A big mistake!

Using a dataset without checking its resolution, date, projection or license — then drawing conclusions from it. Always verify metadata and cite your sources.

Which source for which data? — a quick check

Classroom activity

For each need below, name the source you would reach for first.

1. You need global administrative boundaries (countries, provinces) for a map.

Natural Earth Data — clean global vector boundaries; Diva-GIS is a good alternative for country-level downloads.

2. You need recent land cover for Pakistan at 10 m resolution.

ESRI Landcover Explorer (LULC 2017–2025) — global 10 m land cover, including the Provinces of Pakistan product.

3. You need daily temperature and solar radiation for an evapotranspiration study.

NASA POWER — weather and climate data designed for agricultural and hydrological applications.

4. You need global soil data at 1 km resolution.

FAO Soils Portal — the Harmonized World Soil Database v2.0.

5. You need street-level road data for a city in Pakistan.

OpenStreetMap — download a country extract from GeoFabrik for offline use.

6. You need free satellite imagery (e.g. Landsat or Sentinel scenes).

USGS Earth Explorer (Landsat) or the Sentinel Scientific Data Hub (Sentinel-1/2).

7. You need irrigation infrastructure data for Sindh.

Sindh Irrigation GIS Portal — explore the site and fill out the survey form as instructed in class.

8. You need evapotranspiration and water productivity data for an irrigation study.

WaPOR (FAO) — the Water Productivity Open-access Portal, with evapotranspiration, biomass and water productivity layers.

9. You need drought monitoring maps for Pakistan.

PakDMS — the Pakistan Drought Management System (IWMI/PMD), which maps drought conditions using WaPOR data.

10. You need long-term global climate reanalysis data (e.g. ERA5) for a study.

Copernicus Climate Data Store (CDS) — global reanalysis, seasonal forecasts and climate projections.

Key takeaways

  • Primary data is collected from scratch; secondary data already exists — most projects combine both.
  • GPS estimates position from range measurements: Range = c × (t/2), using at least 4 satellites.
  • Remote sensing measures electromagnetic energy reflected or emitted from objects; satellites differ in orbit, resolution, revisit, swath, wavelengths and more.
  • Follow collection protocols (sampling, standardization, bias, consent) and use protocols (citation, ethics, legality, quality checks).
  • Many free data sources exist: Esri Open Data Hub, Landcover Explorer, Natural Earth, USGS Earth Explorer, OSM/GeoFabrik, Sentinel, NASA POWER, Copernicus CDS, Diva-GIS, FAO Soils, WaPOR.
  • For Pakistan: Sindh Irrigation GIS Portal, province-level land cover (10 m), Data For Pakistan and PakDMS (drought monitoring).
