1. Satellite data for natural hazards

Satellite data and geospatial techniques support hazard planning and risk management across the disaster management cycle. Sensors fall into two broad families:

  • Optical sensors — record reflected sunlight; limited by clouds and darkness.
  • Radar (SAR) sensors — active microwave sensors that operate day and night and penetrate cloud cover.
Satellite / productTypeKey characteristicsDisaster applications
LANDSAT Optical (multispectral) The longest-running exercise in collecting multispectral data — Landsat 1 launched in 1972. Studying land-surface change.
Sentinel-1 SAR Radar (SAR) Radar observations operate day and night and penetrate cloud cover. Flood-extent mapping; earthquake-related ground-deformation analysis.
Sentinel-2 MSI Optical (multispectral) 13 spectral bands at 10–60 m resolution. Mapping of water, vegetation, snow and land-cover change; glacial-lake monitoring; burn-scar assessment.
RADARSAT Radar (SAR) Efficient and effective satellite sensor. Assessing flood damages and oil seeps.
MODIS Terra & Aqua Optical (multispectral) Daily coverage. Rapid disaster mapping.
VIIRS active-fire data Optical (thermal) Near-real-time thermal hotspot detections at 375 m resolution. Complement MODIS; support wildfire monitoring.
GPM IMERG Precipitation estimates Multi-satellite precipitation estimates at half-hourly intervals. Assessment of extreme rainfall, flood-triggering conditions, and drought.
OPERA Dynamic Surface Water Extent Radar-derived Sentinel-1-derived water maps at 30 m resolution. Monitoring changing surface-water extent; flood assessment requires comparison with normal or pre-event water conditions.
Copernicus Global Flood Monitoring Radar-derived Automatically processes Sentinel-1 radar observations. Worldwide satellite-based flood monitoring.

2. Flood monitoring — Pakistan case studies

Pakistan Flood 2010

Satellite imagery captured the scale of the 2010 floods — the worst in Pakistan’s history. Examples include the inundation of Qambar Shahdadkot and the comparison of the Guddu Barrage before and after the event:

  • Pre-flood: June 02, 2010
  • Post-flood: August 12, 2010

Rapid flood mapping by SUPARCO

During the 2010 floods, SUPARCO produced a series of flood/rain extent maps (31 July – 21 September 2010) covering the Indus system from Warsak, Tarbela and Mangla dams to the barrages of Chashma, Guddu, Sukkur and Kotri — showing the direction of flood water as it moved downstream.

Sindh Dewatering Program

Daily flood extent maps were produced by utilizing data from remote sensing satellites to support the dewatering program in Sindh.

Flood 2012

A MODIS image of September 13, 2012 showed the flood extent during the 2012 monsoon floods.

Pakistan Floods 2025

VIIRS false-color images showed flooding along major rivers. Compare September 9, 2025 with the same date in 2023.

GIS task

Overlay inundation with settlements and cropland. Image link: NASA Earth Observatory (2025).

3. Flood modeling and damage assessment

Flood modeling using satellite data and GIS tools

Satellite-derived flood extents feed hydraulic models to simulate flood behavior and support planning.

Damage assessment using satellite data and GIS tools

Damage assessment is performed by overlaying the flooding extent on elements at risk — buildings, roads, cropland and other exposed assets.

Valencia Flash Floods — 2024

Landsat 8 observed inundation on 30 October 2024. Urban land, farms, and coastal wetlands were affected.

GIS task

Compare flood extent with drainage and transport routes. Image link: NASA Earth Observatory (2024).

4. Cyclone monitoring

Satellite imagery documented the impact of Cyclone TC 02A, which hit Thatta District on May 20, 1999:

  • Prior-cyclone satellite image of February 1999 showed the Tidal Link area.
  • The 1998 breach and subsequent breaches were visible in the imagery.
  • Post-cyclone satellite image of November 1999 showed deteriorated mangrove forests in the Tidal Link area, Thatta District, Sindh.

5. Air quality — aerosol optical depth

Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) maps for 2006–2007 show the distribution of atmospheric aerosols — useful for air-quality monitoring and health-risk assessment.

6. Snow cover and glacier monitoring

  • Snow cover mapping — annual snow-cover maps for May 2004–2008 track seasonal and inter-annual variation in glacial snow cover.
  • Gangotri Glacier retreat — ASTER imagery provided estimates of the retreat of the Gangotri Glacier in India (courtesy of Jesse Allen, Earth Observatory, USA).
  • Annual variation in glacial snow cover — time series of snow cover support water-resource and GLOF-risk assessment.

7. Glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs)

Thame GLOF, Nepal — 2024

On 16 August, a rock avalanche triggered two lake outbursts. Satellite images, drone surveys, and fieldwork reconstructed the event.

Lesson

Small lakes can produce large, cascading hazards.

Thame: mapping downstream risk

  • Map the lakes, unstable slopes and connected river channels.
  • Overlay settlements, bridges and hydropower facilities.
  • Use field checks and modelling to assess evacuation options.

Image link: ICIMOD: Thame study (2025).

Rasuwa Flood, Nepal — 2026

A major flood struck the Nepal–China border on 26 August.

  • Assess upstream changes and exposed infrastructure downstream.
  • Cause assessment is preliminary — do not assume a confirmed GLOF.

Source: ICIMOD event updates (2026).

10. From satellite evidence to decisions

  • Remote sensing shows where conditions have changed.
  • GIS identifies the people and infrastructure exposed.
  • Field evidence, uncertainty, and vulnerability guide action.
Class discussion

Which map supports which decision?

Key takeaways

  • Optical sensors (LANDSAT, Sentinel-2, MODIS, VIIRS) and radar sensors (Sentinel-1, RADARSAT) complement each other — radar operates day and night and penetrates cloud cover.
  • During the Pakistan 2010 floods, SUPARCO produced daily flood-extent maps from satellite data, supporting the Sindh Dewatering Program.
  • Damage assessment overlays the flooding extent on elements at risk — buildings, roads and cropland.
  • Snow-cover and glacier monitoring support GLOF risk assessment — small lakes can produce large, cascading hazards.
  • Sentinel-1 radar measured ground displacement in the Türkiye–Syria 2023 earthquakes; RADARSAT detected a 36.85 sq km oil spill in 2003.
  • Remote sensing shows where conditions have changed; GIS identifies who and what is exposed; field evidence and vulnerability guide action.
